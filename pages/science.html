The problem

The success rate of clinical trials is notoriously low: 90% of clinical programmes don't progress past Stage I, and most of this failure is due to safety or efficacy issues. Open Targets was founded to change that.

Clinical trials outcomes are improved for therapies backed by genetic data. Through large-scale genomic experiments and the development of innovative computational techniques, we aim to build a base of genetic knowledge that researchers can use to select the best targets for drug discovery.

What we do

The consortium works pre-competitively to bridge the gap between pharmaceutical companies and not-for-profit research institutes, allowing the partners to apply their combined knowledge and expertise to our research projects.

Since its founding, Open Targets consortium has made significant contributions to understanding the relationships between targets and diseases, established leading informatics tools such as the Open Targets Platform, and contributed to global initiatives such as the Cancer Dependency Map.

Research Programme

Linking targets to diseases &
improving target selection for drug discovery

Our research programme generates and analyses data to connect targets to diseases, assess the strength of that evidence, and prioritise the most promising candidates for drug discovery.

We combine large-scale genomic experiments with high-throughput functional assays in the most relevant cellular systems, working across therapeutic areas of common interest to our partners: Oncology, Neurodegeneration, and Immunology & Inflammation. All three are areas of significant unmet need, where genomic approaches can make a real difference.

Our results and data are shared openly with the broader scientific community and feed into the Open Targets Platform where they can be analysed alongside other public data.

  Target Identification

Define targets with significant associations to disease biology, using whole-genome approaches that assess candidates in a comprehensive genome-wide context.

  Target Prioritisation

Refine the list on the strength of association evidence together with additional target and disease parameters, so effort goes where it is most likely to translate.

Informatics

Core informatics and data generation pipelines

Our core bioinformatics work brings together the data relevant to targets so that drug discovery scientists can explore it simply and seamlessly. We cover many data types relevant to human disease biology and target identification, using expertise from Open Targets partners and beyond.

Flagship resource

Open Targets Platform

Our approach to target identification and prioritisation is brought to life in the Platform, which integrates public data relevant to the association between targets and diseases and provides additional data and tools for prioritisation. It is an open source project, and continues to evolve as we bring new features and data to bear.

Bioinformatics services and tools launched by Open Targets

Several databases and methods that are now central to bioinformatics research were launched through Open Targets projects. Some examples include:

eQTL Catalogue

The eQTL Catalogue aims to provide uniformly processed gene expression and splicing QTLs from all available public studies on human.
Perturbation Catalogue

A curated database that brings together data from various genetic perturbation experiments, making it easier for researchers to study how modifying genes or proteins affects biological function across various biological and molecular contexts.

GWAS Catalog

A consistent, searchable, visualisable and freely available database of SNP-trait associations, and one of the largest repositories of full genome-wide summary statistics.

ProtVar

A resource to investigate SNV missense variation in humans by bringing together structural, functional, population, and disease annotations.
